How it works

HamBBS works like e-mail for radio amateurs. Your messages live on a BBS, and this program keeps a copy of them on your computer.

This program is only connected to the BBS while it syncs. Everything else works offline:

  1. Write a message. It goes to the Outbox; nothing is sent yet.
  2. Press Sync now. In one connection the client sends your outbox and brings home new mail and bulletins.
  3. Read at your own pace. Over radio, you never wait for the BBS while you read.

Nothing happens by itself: press Sync now whenever you want to send or check for new mail. The Sync page shows the result of the last sync.

"Personal" does not mean secret. Over radio anyone can listen, and the sysop can read everything on the BBS. See On the air.

Addressing

Personal messages

  • LA1ABC: a user of your own BBS.
  • LA1ABC@LA9BBS: a user whose home BBS is LA9BBS. Your BBS forwards the message there, possibly through other BBSes on the way.

You need to know the recipient's home BBS, the one they sync with. Ask them, or look at a message they sent you: the last node in its Path is their home BBS. Reply fills in the right address for you.

If your BBS has no route to that node, the message comes back as rejected with the reason "no route". Ask your sysop which BBSes can be reached.

Bulletins

  • TECH: the TECH area on your own BBS only.
  • TECH@ALL: TECH on every BBS in the network.
  • TECH@LA, TECH@EU…: TECH on BBSes that carry that region. Which regions exist depends on how the sysops have connected their BBSes.

Use an existing area if one fits; the Write page suggests the areas you have seen. Choose the smallest audience that makes sense: a network-wide bulletin goes over the air from every BBS to the next.

Reading and fetching

To save airtime, a sync can bring only the header of a message: sender, subject and size. Such messages are marked header only.

  • Open the message and press Fetch text on next sync. The text arrives with your next sync.
  • In Settings you choose what always comes in full: personal mail, and bulletins in the areas you list under "Always fetch full text".
  • "Bulletin areas to follow" limits which bulletins you hear about at all.

On a personal message, Reply answers the sender. On a bulletin you can reply privately to the author, or reply in the area so everyone sees it.

Unread messages are shown in bold. Use Mark unread to keep a message for later.

The outbox

  • queued: waiting for the next sync. You can still remove it.
  • sent: the BBS has accepted it. From there, the BBS forwards it to other BBSes if needed. That can take a while, because BBSes call each other only every so often.
  • rejected: the BBS refused it. The reason is shown below the subject. Fix the problem, write the message again, and remove the rejected one.

Your account

Your account on the BBS is your callsign and a password. On most BBSes, new users just choose a password in Settings, and the first sync creates the account. Some sysops add users by hand instead.

The password itself never leaves this computer. The client proves that you know it with a signature that is only valid for that one connection.

If you forget your password, ask the sysop to reset it. Then enter the new one in Settings.

Without the client, you can also connect with an ordinary packet or telnet terminal. Type H for the list of commands.

On the air

  • Everything is public. Amateur radio rules forbid encryption and anything that hides the meaning. Personal mail is readable by anyone who listens.
  • No business and nothing you would not say on the air.
  • You are responsible for what is sent under your callsign.
  • Keep it short on slow links. A 1200 baud packet link moves roughly 100 bytes per second, so a long letter or a big list of always-fetched areas makes every sync slow.
  • Plain text only. There are no attachments.

Updating this program

New versions are on the Releases page; download hambbs-client for your system.

  1. Close this program: press Ctrl+C in its window, or close the window.
  2. Replace hambbs-client (hambbs-client.exe on Windows) with the new one, in the same folder.
  3. Start it again.

Your settings ({{.Data.Config}}) and the {{.Data.DataDir}} folder with your messages and outbox are kept. You don't need to update at the same time as your BBS.
